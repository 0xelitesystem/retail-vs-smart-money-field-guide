# Glossary

Short definitions of the terms used across this guide. These are educational definitions, not advice.

**Retail** Individual investors trading their own money, typically in smaller size through ordinary brokerage accounts.

**Institutional** Entities investing pooled or mandated capital, such as funds and insurers, subject to disclosure rules individuals are not.

**Smart money** An informal label for participants assumed to be better informed. Not a data field or legal category, and often wrong.

**Volume** The count of shares or contracts traded in a period. Measures activity, not direction.

**Open interest** The number of outstanding derivative contracts. Rising open interest does not reveal whether positions are bets or hedges.

**Order flow** The sequence and size of orders. Buyer or seller initiation is usually an estimate based on price relative to the bid and ask.

**Dark pool** A venue where trades execute away from lit exchanges. Prints appear after the fact without participant identity or reason.

**Hedge** A position taken to offset risk in another position. Looks directional in the data but expresses risk management, not a price view.

**Filing** A periodic regulatory report of holdings. Useful but lagged, and usually incomplete on shorts and derivatives.

**Trade classification** A model that labels trades as buyer- or seller-initiated. An estimate, not a measurement.

**Positioning** A general term for how participants are arranged long or short. Inferred from incomplete data and easy to overstate.
