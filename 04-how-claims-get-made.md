# How a positioning claim gets built

A claim like "smart money is loading up here" is assembled from raw data through a chain of interpretation. Seeing the chain shows you where it can break.

## The chain

1. A raw observation: a large off-exchange print, a jump in open interest, a filing.
2. A classification: the print is labeled institutional, the open interest is labeled new longs.
3. An attribution of intent: the institution is said to be accumulating because it expects the price to rise.
4. A narrative: this becomes "smart money is bullish," often with a confident tone and a chart.

Each step adds an assumption. The raw observation is usually sound. The classification is an estimate. The attribution of intent is a guess. The narrative is presentation.

## Where it breaks

The break is almost always at step three. The data supports "a large trade occurred." It does not support "the trader expects the price to rise," because the same trade fits hedging, rebalancing, unwinding, or forced action. A confident narrative hides the weakest link by skipping straight from observation to intent.

## How to read one

When you encounter a positioning claim, walk it back down the chain. What was actually observed? How was it classified, and by whom? What intent is being attributed, and what else would fit the same data? By the time you reach the raw observation, most claims have shrunk to "something traded," which is true and not actionable on its own.
