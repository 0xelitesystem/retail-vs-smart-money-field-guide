# A skeptical checklist

Before believing any claim about what retail or smart money is doing, run it through these questions. If a claim cannot survive them, set it aside.

1. What was actually measured? Name the raw observation, not the narrative.
2. As of when? Check whether the data is current or lagged by weeks.
3. Who is the actor, specifically? "Smart money" is not an answer.
4. Could this be a hedge? A large position is consistent with protection, not just conviction.
5. What else fits the same data? List at least two non-directional explanations.
6. Is the metric estimated or measured? Trade classification and sentiment scores are models.
7. Where are the counterexamples? If only confirming cases are shown, suspect selection.
8. What would change my mind? A claim that nothing could disprove is a story, not analysis.
9. Who benefits if I believe this? Attention, subscriptions, and order flow are motives.
10. What am I about to do with this, and what does it cost me if it is wrong?

The goal is not cynicism. It is calibration. Most positioning claims are not lies; they are overconfident readings of incomplete data. This list pulls the confidence back to where the evidence actually sits.
