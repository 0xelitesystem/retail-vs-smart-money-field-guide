# Common misreadings

The same errors recur in nearly every confident positioning take. Recognizing them protects you from acting on noise.

## Confusing a hedge with a bet

A large protective position looks identical in the data to a large directional position. A fund buying downside protection is not bearish on the underlying in the way a headline implies. Hedges are a major share of large derivative flows, and they invert the naive reading.

## Treating stale as current

Filings and many reports describe the past. A holding shown today may have been sold weeks ago. Building a present-tense story on past-tense data is one of the most common and least examined mistakes.

## Assuming one actor

"Smart money" is thousands of entities with conflicting views, mandates, and timelines. Aggregating them into a single intelligent agent that agrees with your thesis is a story, not a finding.

## Survivorship in the examples

Narratives are illustrated with the cases where a large trade preceded a big move. The cases where it preceded nothing, or the opposite, are not shown. The examples select themselves.

## Mistaking activity for information

Volume and open interest measure activity. Activity is not a signal of direction. A busy tape tells you people disagree enough to trade, which is always true.

## Anchoring on a single metric

Any one metric can be made to support a story. The discipline is to ask what would have to be true across independent sources, and to notice when only one cherry-picked number is offered.
